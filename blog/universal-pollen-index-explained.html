What is the Universal Pollen Index?

Reference · updated August 2026

The Universal Pollen Index (UPI) is a 0–5 scale used by Google's Pollen API to describe how much pollen is in the air. It is reported per pollen type — tree, grass, weed — and per individual plant, at roughly 1 km resolution.

The six levels

UPICategoryWhat it means in practice
0NoneNothing of that type in the air.
1Very LowFine for almost everyone.
2LowOnly the very sensitive will notice.
3ModerateMost people allergic to that pollen will feel it.
4HighA rough day if you react to what is out there.
5Very HighStay in if you can.

How it is built

Google describes the UPI as a representation of pollen level that accounts for the concentration per plant model, many different local pollen indices used worldwide, and the allergenicity of different pollen types — that is, how strongly a given pollen tends to provoke symptoms. It is a modelled prediction of grains per cubic metre, not a direct count.

Two consequences follow. First, it is a forecast, so it can be wrong. Second, because allergenicity is folded in, a 3 for ragweed and a 3 for pine are not the same number of grains — they are a similar amount of trouble.

Why you cannot convert it to the 0–12 scale

Several US apps use a different 0–12 index. It is tempting to divide by 2.4 and move on. That does not work: across 175 place-days in 35 US cities, the best possible straight-line conversion removed only 14% of the variation, and barely beat ignoring the other source altogether. The full comparison is here.

Practical rule: never compare a number from one app to a number from another. Compare an app only against itself, over time.

Reading a UPI number well

  • Check the type, not the total. Grass 4 matters enormously if you react to grass and not at all if you do not.
  • Watch the direction. A rise from 2 to 4 is more informative than the 4 on its own.
  • Expect rain to knock it down. Heavy rain washes pollen out of the air, so a wet day usually under-runs its forecast.
  • In season matters. Google flags whether a plant is in its season; an out-of-season plant showing a 1 is usually background.

Pollenary shows the UPI per plant per day, so you can see a Ragweed 4 sitting next to a Tree 1 instead of one blended score.

Pollen levels are a forecast, not a medical measurement. Pollenary is not a medical device and does not diagnose or treat any condition. Talk to a doctor about your allergies.
